Exploring the genome

Computational and experimental genomics for understanding human variation

We combine modern genomics, high-throughput experiments, and computational methods to study gene regulation, repetitive DNA, somatic variation, and the functional consequences of genetic variation.

From the principal investigator

Alan P. Boyle, Ph.D.

Understanding gene regulation at the transcriptional level is critical to understanding complex biological systems and human disease. In virtually all organisms, distinct combinations of transcription factors collaborate through a regulatory code to control the expression of individual genes. This code is complex, distributed across cis-regulatory elements, and not readily apparent from sequence alone.

Our research aims to define this regulatory code, understand how regulatory and repetitive sequences vary among people and cells, and connect those differences to molecular function and disease.
